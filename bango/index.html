Bango Case study Prediction markets · Avalanche

Rebuilding Bango for everyone.

Bango is a prediction market for micro-communities: bet on anything your circle cares about. As Design Lead I created the brand and led the relaunch, turning a crypto-native trading screen into something anyone can use, with social login, no gas fees and an XP economy.

01 The problem

Prediction markets were built for traders, not communities

Big prediction markets list the questions they choose, need a crypto wallet before you can do anything, and charge gas on every bet. A group of friends, a campus or a fan club can't easily bet on the things only they care about.

Bango's team named four problems to fix. Two were mine to solve through design; the other two shaped what the interface had to explain.

Design

Complex UX

Wallet-first onboarding and gas fees kept anyone outside crypto from placing a first bet.

Design

No niche markets

Market creation was centralised, so micro-community questions never got listed.

Product

Liquidity barrier

Creating a market needed deep liquidity, which a unified liquidity vault was built to solve.

Product

Slow resolution

Manual oracles were slow and didn't scale; an AI oracle resolves markets faster.

Problem list from Bango's own breakdown on X, Aug 2025.

What I owned
  • Brand identity: logo, palette, typography and the brand kit
  • Relaunch redesign across two production versions
  • Discovery, market and profile pages on web and mobile
  • Onboarding with social login instead of wallet-first
  • XP and Reward Center: quests, referrals, leaderboard
  • Social and launch graphics, plus a 60-second game concept
Constraints
  • Users new to crypto had to place a first bet without learning what a wallet is
  • Odds, prices and countdowns had to be readable at a glance on a phone
  • Markets range from Bitcoin prices to campus gossip, so cards needed to carry any topic
  • A small founding team shipping fast between launches
01
Brand identity

A number that holds a community together

Bango means "number" in Japanese, so the mark is an 8. Its soft, rounded corners hold everything in place, the way the product holds micro-communities together. Deep black stands for the boldness of truth-seekers; one bright blue adds trust and keeps it friendly for beginners.

02
The relaunch

From a trading screen to a feed anyone can read

V1 looked like an exchange: a wall of identical cards, an activity log of trades and a login that meant "connect your wallet". For V2 I rebuilt discovery around one question at a time, a live price and two big buttons: Up or Down.

Connect wallet to startLog in with a social account; no gas
Yes / No percentage barsUp / Down buttons with a price in cents
Raw trade activity logCategories, live markets and a USDC / XP toggle
03
Market page

Everything you need to decide, on one screen

A bet is a decision under time pressure. The market page answers three questions in order: what's the target, where is it now, and how long do I have, with the buy panel always in reach.

  1. To beatThe target price the market resolves against.
  2. CurrentThe live price and its change, in blue so it reads as "now".
  3. CountdownTime left, in red, the only alarm colour on the page.
  4. Recurring time slotsHourly markets on the same question, so there's always a next round.
  5. Buy / Sell panelUp and Down priced in cents, quick-add amounts and the potential return before you commit.
  6. Description, rules and commentsResolution criteria in plain words, and a thread to argue your side.
04
Onboarding

Log in. Pick a market. Send it.

The single biggest change: no wallet and no gas before the first bet. Social login replaced wallet-first onboarding, and after Bango integrated 0xGasless, predictions cost no gas at all. The interface never asks a newcomer to understand the chain.

Log in

Sign in with a social account. No wallet setup, no seed phrase up front.

Pick a market

Categories and live markets surface something worth betting on in seconds.

Send it

Choose Up or Down, set an amount, confirm. No gas prompt, no network switch.

05
XP economy

A way to play before you pay

Newcomers needed a reason to try a bet without risking money. The XP system lets them earn points from daily quests and referrals, then bet with XP instead of USDC, switched with one toggle in the header. Leaderboards turn it into a game.

06
Mobile

Colour-coded, so a feed of questions never blurs together

On a phone every card is the same size, so I gave each market a soft colour glow taken from its own image. You can tell a Porsche question from a campus party at a glance, and live markets expand to show the price chart right in the feed.

Decision

One card component carries every topic. Colour comes from the market image, so new markets get a distinct look without extra design work.

Share card

A shareable PnL card, so wins spread back into the community.

07
Exploration

60-second Bango

A faster format: will Solana go up or down in the next 60 seconds? I designed the full loop, from choosing a side to the resolving state and the win, with streaks and multipliers to keep people playing.

NoteThis was a design exploration from October 2025. I'm showing it for the interaction design, not as a shipped feature.

04 Reflection

What I'd keep, change and do next

What worked
  • Removing the wallet from the first five minutes
  • Up / Down in cents is easier to read than yes / no percentages
  • XP gave newcomers a no-risk first bet
What I'd do next
  • Usability-test the market page with people who have never used crypto
  • Surface resolution rules earlier, before the first bet
  • Make market creation as simple as betting, for micro-communities
What I learned
  • The best crypto UX hides the crypto
  • A brand is a system, not a logo: it has to work in a feed card at 40px
